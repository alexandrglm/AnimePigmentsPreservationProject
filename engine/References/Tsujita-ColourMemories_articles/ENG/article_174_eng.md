**Episode 1: The Bell of Fate Rings**  
Storyboard: Ikuhara Kunihiko, Direction: Nakamura Akiko, Color Direction (色指定): Tsujita Kunio

First, Episode 1. Episode 1 is important anyway. We have to properly present "this is what we're doing!" first. Story, direction, animation too, but altogether showing "screen basics." This becomes Episode 1's most important point. For *Mawaru Penguindrum*, we considered the Takakura house living room and kitchen scenes as "screen basics."

The first art boards submitted were Episode 1's opening bed, Takakura house living room, and kitchen. The bed is also an important key scene, but more of a place to show imagistically. The center of acting is naturally Takakura house daily acting. Firmly deciding and creating this, burning this visual balance into viewers' minds is important.

So *Mawaru Penguindrum's* main character colors were decided on this living room background board.

Director Ikuhara's order was "clear feeling." My image was the same direction - being careful not to become ambiguous by over-aiming or look dirty by lowering saturation. And the delivered art board's vividness and wonderful hand-drawn density feeling. "Good!" I began creating colors with good motivation.

Kanba and Shoma's hair colors - red and blue directions - were from the director. Since they were supposedly "twins," I tried creating "twin-ness" by matching and unifying eye colors. But actually at this point, though they were "brothers" and "siblings," there was "actually..." so I hoped for good misleading visuals and colors.

For Himari, I initially considered darker black-ish or dark brown hair with sickly pale skin, but the director said "more fragile feeling..." so hair became light, and we modified hair main lines to bright, light brown. Bright, fragile-feeling skin and hair, white-based blouse with vivid light blue skirt. This light blue - I thought slightly more subdued might be good, but the director demanded vividness "to the limit" for that balance. Light blue tends to develop intensely on HD screens, so quite nerve-wracking colors. And boots.

These boots were another struggle. Sheepskin-like boot image was immovable from the start, but basic main line colors made outline lines too strong for boot colors, creating "unbeautiful" balance. So ultimately, I decided to brighten boot main line colors too. Color tracing for hair (eyebrows) plus boot main lines. This is quite troublesome in coloring work. Certainly becomes difficult, but this good balance was non-negotiable. So I decided to go with this color tracing.

When general color balance was decided, "Tsujita-kun, a little consultation..." said Director Ikuhara. "Could we make the eye feeling more Hoshino-san-like?" This time, character concepts were requested from Hoshino Lily-san, and the consultation was whether we could put Hoshino-san's color illustration atmosphere into main story visuals. Hoshino-san's drawn eyes have dark pupils with deep, beautiful, sexy bleeding. I was conscious of that, but Nishii-san's animation character artwork details alone didn't work well. But adding more complex details and highlights to artwork itself might cause painful results if character animation wavered.

So what to do? Slightly tighter and darker than initially decided, plus one more processing. Using eye pupil colors, adding gradation brushes to eyes that gradually darken upward. We asked photography staff to add this. This was how I compiled it as a compromise. Uniformly adding this processing to main characters. Naturally, some cuts have additional ingenuity.

Characters were roughly decided like this.

Oh yes, penguins (laugh). Penguins are naturally that color (laugh). I tried making blackish-colored ones too, but they were lackluster. Long ago there was a Suntory commercial campaign called "Penguin's Bar" with penguins this blue. That color image seems imprinted on both me and Director Ikuhara (laugh). Incidentally, the blackish penguin color I tried making was later inherited by Esmeralda.

Like that, Episode 1 main story.

As written earlier, the episode presents "screen basics" of "this is what we're doing!" I thought it important to impress important visuals for stories developing over 2 cours (all 24 episodes) in the first episode. So Episode 1 basically allocated more "normal" colored scenes. Also, firmly showing important scenes (special coloring scenes) appearing throughout at key points. These two points were first important.

So basic "Takakura house living room," subway interiors, aquarium, souvenir shops were firmly shown in "normal colors." Conversely, hospital examination room's bluish scenes, scenes where Himari stops breathing, and morgue's strong evening scenes were also created with colors to remain strongly impressive. Anyway, screen tempo and contrast. I intended to avoid brightness changes and shadows by time of day in Episode 1 as much as possible, but naturally rain scenes had brightness adjustments matching backgrounds.

My series-wide design plan was to build relatively natural-looking color design for the first half cour, then deploy various tricky color usage from when the story begins moving significantly in the middle. It's 2 cours after all. First, creating screen balance that lets stories smoothly enter viewers was essential.

Well, Episode 1 was exciting anyway. Just enjoyable. How will we proceed? And ah, so this is how the animation becomes! Color direction input and coloring completion inspection work were endlessly fun. Oh yes, as Episode 1's small detail, the commuter pass holder delivered to #2 - that color is my actually used commuter pass holder (laugh). I was commuting to Mitaka's Brain's Base with that pass holder and Suica commuter pass (laugh). When you do color direction yourself, such fun is possible - enjoyable. Self-satisfaction though (laugh).

Even so, those two boys... (bitter laugh). Those were colors decided in the moment - if I'd known from the start, I would have decided more carefully (bitter laugh).
